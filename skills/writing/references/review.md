# Reviewing a draft

Provisional, 14 November 2026. Read this before reviewing someone else's draft, or before checking your own at the author's request. It covers analytical and persuasive writing with a brief behind it; investment memos are the first intended use.

The aim is a draft that does what the author's brief and plan set out to do, for its reader. The checks below are ways of finding where it does not. A draft can pass all of them and still fail the brief, so report what you find against the brief, and treat a clean result as a valid result.

## What the reviewer needs

Most defects in a draft are only visible against something outside it. Ask for these before starting, and say which you did not get:

- **The brief and the plan:** the question being answered, the argument the author intends, and any house format or judging criteria.
- **The primary sources** behind the draft's material claims.
- **The author's own wording**, where parts of the draft are theirs.

Do not take the writer's account of why the draft works. A reviewer who has not seen the drafting conversation, ideally a different model family, catches more; the mechanics are the same as [independent code review](../../code-quality/references/review.md). Without the brief and plan, limit the review to the source and sentence checks and say so.

## Checks

### Against the sources

1. **Each material claim reconciles to a primary document.** Record what the document establishes, how certain it is, when it takes effect and what it depends on. Flag figures that are assumptions, and figures that differ between documents.
2. **Each inference is as strong as its evidence.** Ask of a neat conclusion whether the source substantiates it or only fits it. Overstated doubt is the same fault in the other direction.
3. **The evidence is current** for a field that moves. Search before dropping a point for lack of recent support.

### Against the plan

4. **One argument governs the piece.** Each section advances it, and the terms that carry it recur wherever the argument turns. Varying those terms for elegance hides the thread.
5. **Each mechanism is shown.** For a causal claim, the reader can tell who does what, and why their incentive changes. Two questions find most gaps: "as opposed to what?" and "when would this not hold?"
6. **Coverage has not displaced the argument.** Procedure, exhaustive lists and side distinctions are the first things to cut when the main point stops showing.
7. **Consequential specifics survive.** Names, figures and a source's own phrases have not been averaged into general statements.

### In the sentence

8. **Actors and verbs** replace abstract nouns that hide who acts. Keep an abstraction that states a clear relationship.
9. **Numbers have denominators**, terms are defined at first use, and parallel things are named in parallel.
10. **Nothing defensive has been added:** no exaggerated reading introduced in order to reject it, no hedge the form would not use, no labels or templates nobody asked for.
11. **The ending states the claim or decision** in the argument's own terms. A contrast or a list of three in the last sentence usually stands in for one.

### Form and voice

12. **The house form is followed:** the order of sections, what leads, the person ("we"), and how much qualification the genre carries.
13. **The author's wording is intact** wherever they wrote it. Report a disagreement; do not overwrite.

## Reporting

- Quote the sentence and ask the smallest question that exposes the gap, or name what is missing. Prefer this to rewriting, unless the author asks for alternatives.
- Flag over-explanation as readily as under-explanation. A connection the reader can make unaided should not be announced.
- Say what you checked, what you could not check, and which findings rest on a source you read as opposed to an impression.
- Do not aim for a number of findings.

## After a fix

- Reread the sentences on either side and the transition into the next paragraph. Fixes often cause the next defect.
- Check the fix against the plan, and against the comment that prompted it.
- Treat one correction as one correction. Generalising it into a rule needs the author's agreement.
- A convincing account of what went wrong is not the repair. The next draft is the evidence.

## Not yet covered

- **Outcomes.** These checks test reasoning, and an investment memo's verdict arrives years later. One candidate: record a dated prediction with a probability for each axis of the memo when the decision is made, for passes as well as investments, and score them as they resolve.
- **Essay style.** Rules transmitted it poorly. Pairs of a model's sentence and the author's revision may work better; untested.
