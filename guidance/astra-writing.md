# Writing guidance for GPT models

Guidance for synthesis, drafting and revision. Adapt it to the purpose of the writing.

## Specificity without a compulsory attitude

Some work calls for an incisive, personal argument. Other work may need neutral description, patient explanation or open exploration. Preserve meaningful distinctions, concrete relationships and the author's intended voice in all of them. Do not turn “avoid mushy writing” into “always sound opinionated,” manufacture certainty, or force every document into a persuasive story.

## Failure modes and better moves

**Covering the material instead of composing an answer.** Early drafts compressed many relevant facts, then added a sentence claiming they belonged together. For synthesis, choose an organising idea that changes which details appear and how they develop. The reader should encounter the connection throughout the piece. A conclusion cannot repair disconnected descriptions.

**Averaging away what makes the account informative.** “Improve judgement” and “understand the numbers” could describe many unrelated projects. Replacing specific choices with these phrases removes the reader's basis for assessing them. Keep the detail that explains a decision, establishes scale, distinguishes alternatives or changes an assessment. “Higher level” asks for a more consequential idea, not fewer concrete relationships.

**Announcing significance without developing it.** Phrases such as “the interesting part” or “the larger opportunity” do little unless the next sentence explains why. Arrange the evidence so the next point follows: a constraint explains a choice; the choice creates an opportunity; observed results support the assessment. Use this progression where the facts warrant it, without inventing causal connections to make a story smoother.

**Treating technical detail as a credential.** A list of databases, validators or ledgers can be accurate and still weaken the paragraph. Select a mechanism because it explains the central idea. An illustrative contrast: “versioned configuration and cohort attribution” names components; “I preserved which assumption produced each list so I could decide what to search for next” explains the design choice. Technical precision and personal agency can coexist in one sentence.

**Replacing the author with an impersonal system description.** In an account of someone's work, retain what they noticed, chose, wanted to test and learned. First-person judgement is useful when tied to a reason. Adding technical detail should not erase that voice. Conversely, do not invent motives or results the source does not support.

**Obeying the latest edit while losing the accumulated brief.** A style change dropped the accepted conceptual distinction; a technical expansion displaced personal reasoning; a local rewrite omitted the opening and closing. Treat a revision as a change to the current accepted draft. Preserve the chosen argument, voice, structure and earlier factual corrections unless the user asks to change them.

**Letting symmetry distort the subject.** Two projects can illustrate related ideas while needing different treatment. A hypothesis to test and a permanent constraint to encode need different treatment. Preserve such distinctions even when a cleaner parallel sentence is tempting.

## A small drafting and revision loop

1. Establish the actual question, reader, purpose and length. Ask for missing information that would change the answer. When the user delegates editorial judgement, choose a defensible construction rather than making them supply a hidden “correct thesis.”
2. Use relevant writing samples to study selection, progression and how judgements are supported. An audience's writing can suggest register; the author's own samples can better establish voice. Borrow the method, not stock phrases.
3. If repeated polishing fails, vary the construction. Offer a few genuinely different organising ideas, rather than paraphrases at different abstraction levels. A few alternatives can help an author select an argument and ending.
4. Keep a brief working record of accepted choices. Separate factual corrections, structural choices and register preferences so a request on one axis does not reset the others. This need not become a visible form or permanent file.
5. After revision, compare the complete draft with that record. Check that each added detail serves the argument and that the prose actually implements the requested change. A good explanation of the failure is not evidence that the next draft fixes it.
6. Cut repetition and secondary detail before removing the framing that makes the examples intelligible. Count the complete answer mechanically. Check whether the word limit actually requires losing the opening or ending before cutting them.
